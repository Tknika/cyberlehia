CyberLehia is the cybersecurity competition for **Vocational Training (VET) students in the Basque Country**. It is promoted by the Basque Government's Deputy Ministry for Vocational Training (Lanbide Heziketako Sailburuordetza) and CyberZaintza, the Basque Cybersecurity Agency. It is organised together with Tknika (the Basque Centre for Research and Applied Innovation in VET), the Basque Government's Department of Education and FP Euskadi.

It is at the same time a **CTF** (*Capture The Flag*) championship and a meeting point for students, schools and cybersecurity companies. Its goals are to foster a cybersecurity culture in Vocational Training, to showcase the work done in the schools and to motivate students.

{{< note >}}
Taking part is only open to students of Vocational Training schools in the Basque Country. This page explains the competition for anyone outside it who is interested in how it works.
{{< /note >}}

## What is a CTF?

In a *Capture The Flag* competition, teams solve security challenges: finding a vulnerability, analysing a file, breaking a weak encryption, investigating a system… Each solved challenge reveals a hidden code, the *flag*, which the team submits to score points. The challenges cover different topics and levels of difficulty, so they reward both knowledge and creativity.

## Who takes part

Students from the Basque Country's Integrated Vocational Training Schools and from the schools that offer the official Cybersecurity Specialisation, whatever their course. They compete in **teams of up to three**.

In the 2026 edition, **346 students** in **143 teams** took part, from **35 schools** in the three Basque provinces, with the support of **60 teachers**. Most of them study computing and communications, and almost a third were on the cybersecurity specialisation course.

## How the competition works

Each edition runs over a few months, from registration in December to the final in March. It has three phases, and the best teams move on from one to the next.

1. **First qualifying phase: online CTF.** Teams take part from their schools or from home, with only an Internet connection. The challenges come in three levels: guided step-by-step challenges for those trying cybersecurity for the first time, easy challenges to spark curiosity, and advanced challenges for those aiming to win.
2. **Second qualifying phase: three tests.** *Attacking Artificial Intelligence* (getting a language model to reveal a secret it is keeping), *Bug Bounty* (finding security flaws in real systems, as ethical hackers do, and reporting them) and *Show off what you know!* (writing publicly about their experience at CyberLehia). The best five teams reach the final.
3. **The final, in person.** The five finalist teams compete face to face in an Attack/Defence environment and in a surprise challenge revealed on the day. They also present to a jury of cybersecurity professionals an attack they carried out in an earlier phase.

The competition has clear rules of fair play: no attacking the competition's own infrastructure, no sharing *flags* or hints outside the team, and no using artificial intelligence to solve the challenges on its own.

## In the schools

Students usually form their teams with classmates from their own school, and teachers support them: 60 teachers collaborated in the 2026 edition, and each finalist team goes to the final with a teacher from its school. The online phases can be played from the school or from home.

## The final: more than a competition

The final brings together the finalist teams, their schools and cybersecurity companies in a single event. Besides the competition itself, it has talks, technical workshops and company stands, so students can meet the companies directly and see where their studies can take them.

## Editions so far

- **2024 · Bilbao, La Terminal:** first edition.
- **2025 · Bilbao, La Terminal:** second edition, with talks, workshops and company stands at the final.
- **2026 · Donostia / San Sebastián, Tabakalera:** third edition, on 5 March.
- **2027 · Bilbao:** the fourth edition is under way, with the final on 17 March.
